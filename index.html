Content Refresh Prioritization: A Client-Grouped Validation Study on 79M Rows of Production Search Data

Muhammad Ahmed Zia · BS Computer Science, COMSATS University Islamabad · Repository · Contact

Abstract

Which pages should a content team review first, given a limited weekly capacity and thousands of candidates? This paper builds and validates a ranked content-review queue using FlyRank's internship-warehouse dataset, a 78.8-million-row daily production search fact table spanning 70 clients. A Random Forest classifier, trained on first-half-of-month observable signals to predict within-month visibility decline, is compared against a transparent hand-written baseline rule under a client-grouped validation split. The model shows a measured, modest improvement over the baseline (Precision@50 of 0.740 versus 0.560) on held-out clients never seen during training. A parallel audit shows that validating this same model under a naive, ungrouped split would have overstated Precision@50 by 0.200 — a finding this paper treats as being as important as the headline result itself. The output is framed throughout as decision-support for human review, not as an autonomous or causal claim about why any individual page is declining.

Introduction & Problem Statement

Content teams at scale face a simple, unglamorous problem: given thousands of published pages and a small review team, which pages deserve a human's limited attention first? A naive answer — "review whatever looks stale" — ignores real signal sitting in observable search behavior: visibility, click-through rate relative to ranking position, and how those change over time. FlyRank's product already encodes rules like this in its own optimization flags, but those flags are deliberately not shipped in the internship dataset, so this project builds and validates a ranking approach from raw observable signals rather than reproducing an existing rule.

This paper follows FlyRank's own Lane 2: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring — producing a ranked review queue with reason codes, validated against a transparent baseline on the same held-out data, with an explicit accounting of where the validation itself could be misleading.

Why a mid-panel month, not the most recent month: the dataset's final month is a sealed test month by design — it is the natural outcome window for any past-to-future label, and using it to develop label logic would let the future leak into "now." March 2026 was chosen specifically to avoid this.

Excluded, by design, and why (public-safe):

  • FlyRank's own product decision flags (health_score, priority_score, action_type) — not shipped in the release, specifically to prevent a model from simply learning to reproduce an existing rule rather than discovering signal from evidence.
  • Raw client names, domains, URLs, queries, and titles — scrambled before release; this paper never attempts to reconstruct them and reports only pseudonymized hash IDs and aggregate metrics.
  • The _sample table (the sealed final month) — reserved as an unseen test set, never used for label design or feature development.

Methodology

Unit of analysis and label

One row = one content item (content_hash_id), summarized over the analysis month. The label, is_declining, is a proxy: 1 if a page's average daily impressions in the second half of the month are lower than the first half, else 0. This is a current-window proxy, not a true future outcome — a limitation carried through every result in this paper (see Limitations).

Features

All features are computed using only first-half-of-month data, deliberately excluding any second-half signal (including the raw avg_impr_second_half value the label is built from):

FeatureWhy it's knowable at the decision point
impressionsObserved Search Console signal, first half only
avg_positionObserved ranking position, first half only
days_with_impressionsCount of visible days, first half only
content_age_daysFixed at publish time, predates the decision entirely
avg_impr_first_halfBy construction, first half only
ctrDerived from first-half clicks/impressions only

Baseline

A transparent hand-written rule: score = impressions × max(0, tier_avg_ctr − page_ctr), flagging pages with real search volume that under-perform their position tier's expected click-through rate. Two signals behind this rule were checked against real data before use: CTR-by-position-tier (CONFIRMED — CTR falls from 1.24% at top-3 to 0.09% at deep positions, n=11,681–81,988 per tier) and volume-as-a-position-predictor (MIXED — average position does not improve monotonically with impression volume; only the top volume quartile shows a real advantage). The mixed result is treated as a real finding, not a discarded negative: it is why the model uses volume only alongside a real CTR gap, never as a standalone signal.

Validation design

Both baseline and model are scored on the same client-grouped test split. The naive-split path (bottom right) is shown deliberately — it produces an invalid, inflated number, documented in this paper rather than discarded.

Train/test splitting is grouped by client (GroupShuffleSplit, 33 train clients / 11 test clients, confirmed zero client overlap) so that no client's pages appear in both train and test. A random, ungrouped split was deliberately also run as a negative control (see Results) to quantify how much a naive split would have overstated performance.

Leakage audit

An explicit check confirmed: no product decision flags used as features; no label-derived columns (avg_impr_second_half, is_declining) used as features; every feature restricted to first-half-of-month data, matching the window the label itself is built from. An earlier iteration of this model, before this fix, used full-month features against a half-month label and produced an impossible Precision@50 of 1.0 — that failure and its fix are documented in the project's notebook history as a worked example of the leakage lesson, not erased from the record.

Results

All results below are measured on the same client-grouped held-out test split (14,520 rows, 11 clients never seen during training), evaluated with Precision@50 (of the top 50 ranked pages, how many are actually declining) and Average Precision.

MethodPrecision@50Average Precision
Baseline (CTR-gap rule)0.5600.561
Random Forest (validated)0.7400.608
Random Forest, naive ungrouped split (invalid, shown for comparison)0.940—
The naive-split gap is itself a finding. Scoring the identical model on a random (non-client-grouped) split shows 44 overlapping clients between train and test and a Precision@50 of 0.940 — a 0.200 overstatement versus the honest, client-grouped result. This happens because a random split lets the model partly memorize client-specific patterns rather than learn signal that generalizes to an unseen client. Any number from this project that matters for a real decision uses the grouped-split result (0.740), never the naive one.

What the model leans on

Permutation importance on the validated split ranks content_age_days highest, ahead of the baseline's own core signal (CTR gap). This is reported as a directional observation, not a proven causal driver — it is worth testing on a second month before acting on it broadly, since content age confounds are a known risk in comparisons of this kind.

Action distribution (validated model, test split)

Of 14,520 test-set pages: 143 flagged for direct human review, 8,228 for lighter-touch monitoring, 6,149 requiring no action — a workload shape proportional to a small review team's real capacity, not a flag on every page.

Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Proxy label, not ground truth. is_declining compares two halves of one month. Neither the baseline nor the model has access to a true future outcome, so a "correct" ranking means "matches this proxy," not "predicts what will actually happen next."
  • Single month, single snapshot. All results are from March 2026 only. Client mix, seasonality, or platform changes could shift these patterns; nothing here is validated across multiple months.
  • Look-alike patterns not ruled out. A decline in this proxy could reflect consolidation (a sibling URL absorbing traffic), seasonality, or noise rather than a genuine, fixable content problem — this study ranks candidates for review, it does not diagnose cause.
  • Unbalanced client history. Only a subset of clients have 12+ months of history; a single mid-panel month may underrepresent clients who onboarded later in the available window.
  • Not a causal claim. content_age_days ranking highest in permutation importance is an observed, directional pattern on this split — not evidence that age causes decline, and not yet tested on a second month.

Ranked Recommendations

Recommendations follow directly from the validated model's output queue, each carrying one reason code and one action label — never a bundled, ambiguous flag.

1

Review the 143 flagged pages first, in ranked order

Highest predicted decline probability, real search volume (≥500 impressions). Reason codes: declining_visibility_pattern (majority) or ctr_shortfall_at_position (a smaller, CTR-specific subset).

Human review required before any change — see checklist below.

2

Light-touch monitor the 8,228 "monitor" pages

Moderate decline probability or lower volume. Not urgent, but worth a lighter recurring check rather than being ignored entirely.

3

No action needed for the remaining 6,149 pages

Low predicted decline probability on this month's evidence — reviewer time is better spent elsewhere.

Before acting on any "review_content" row, a human must check

  1. Is the page's impression/position history stable (not a brand-new page still settling, not a tracking gap)?
  2. Does a manual look rule out consolidation (a sibling URL absorbing traffic) or seasonality?
  3. Is content_age_days meaningful here, or could content_created_date be stale or missing?

What must never be automated

  • Auto-publishing content changes from this queue — it ranks review candidates only.
  • Auto-emailing a client that their content is "declining" based on model output alone.
  • Using the ranked order as a performance review of content authorship.
  • Depublishing or removing any page based on a flag alone, without the manual checks above.

Monitoring and retrain triggers

  • Retrain monthly as new warehouse partitions become available — this model is validated on March 2026 only.
  • Retrain if Precision@50 on a fresh month drops below 0.55, meaningfully below the validated 0.740.
  • Re-check after any significant client-mix change (new clients onboarded).
  • Track what fraction of "review_content" flags a human actually confirms as real issues weekly — a sharp drop signals drift.
